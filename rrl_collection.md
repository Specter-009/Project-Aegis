# RRL Collection: Project Aegis
## Secure Mobile-Based Anonymous Counseling System for Students of Northern Bukidnon State College

### Subtopic: Digital Mental Health & mHealth Interventions

**Local RRL #1**
Ochoa, J., Crisostomo, A., & Tigno, M. A. (2024). Volunteer online counseling during COVID-19: Insights, practices, and future directions. *Journal of Integrated Psychology*. https://jippublication.com/index.php/jip/article/download/836/633
**Summary:** This study examines the practices and insights from a volunteer-led online counseling initiative in the Philippines, highlighting that the majority of clients were students. It emphasizes the need for formalized digital mental health guidelines and infrastructure in local contexts.

**Local RRL #2**
Ngo, M. G. A. M. (2023). *ACAPP: A Mental Health Application for the Well-Being of Filipino University Students*. University of the Philippines Manila. http://cas.upm.edu.ph:8080/xmlui/handle/123456789/2690
**Summary:** This research details the development and preliminary assessment of a mental health application specifically designed for Filipino university students. It focuses on addressing the unique cultural and psychological needs of the student population through digital tools.

**Local RRL #3**
Bangalan, S., & Agnes, M. C. (2025). A pilot study of the AKBAY mobile app for the mental health of university students. *Journal of Enabling Technologies*. https://www.emerald.com/jet/article/19/2/90/1252321
**Summary:** The study evaluates the AKBAY mobile app's effectiveness in providing mental health support to students in the Philippines. Findings indicate that mobile-based interventions can significantly lower barriers to accessing psychological support.

**Local RRL #4**
Aizon, A., & Punzalan, J. K. (2025). Mobile health application and CBT Workbook on the Psychosocial Well-being among Senior High School Students in Zamboanga City. *Asian Journal of Public Health Practice*. https://journals.lww.com/ajphp/fulltext/2025/01000/mobile_health_application_and_cognitive_behavioral.4.aspx
**Summary:** This intervention study tests the impact of a combined mHealth app and CBT workbook on student well-being. The findings suggest that mobile health tools are effective in improving psychosocial outcomes for Filipino youth.

**Foreign RRL #1**
Salamanca-Sanabria, A., et al. (2023). Perceptions of mHealth interventions for mental disorders in university students in Singapore. *Journal of Medical Internet Research*. https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e44542/
**Summary:** This study explores how university students perceive mHealth tools for mental health prevention. It identifies key themes such as the need for cultural relevance, ease of use, and professional oversight.

**Foreign RRL #2**
Harith, S., et al. (2022). Effectiveness of digital mental health interventions for university students: an umbrella review. *PeerJ*. https://peerj.com/articles/13111/
**Summary:** This umbrella review synthesizes existing meta-analyses to conclude that digital interventions are effective for reducing anxiety and depression symptoms. It notes that efficacy is highest when tools are interactive and evidence-based.

**Foreign RRL #3**
Oliveira, C., et al. (2021). Effectiveness of mobile app-based psychological interventions for college students: a systematic review. *Frontiers in Psychology*. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.647606/full 
**Summary:** This systematic review evaluates mobile psychological interventions for college students. It highlights the potential of apps to bridge treatment gaps in settings with limited traditional resources.

**Foreign RRL #4**
Vereschagin, M., et al. (2024). Effectiveness of the Minder mobile mental health intervention for university students: RCT. *Journal of Medical Internet Research*. https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e54287/
**Summary:** This randomized controlled trial demonstrates the effectiveness of the Minder app in improving mental health outcomes. The study provides robust evidence that structured mobile interventions lead to clinical improvements.

### Subtopic: Anonymity & Privacy in Student Counseling

**Local RRL #5**
Sanchez-Maddela, S. B., & Cruz-Cada, R. D. (2024). Ethical challenges faced by Philippine guidance counselors under the new normal. *Diversitas Journal*. https://diversitasjournal.com.br/diversitas_journal/article/view/2869
**Summary:** Investigates ethical dilemmas in the shift to online counseling in the Philippines, focusing on client confidentiality. It underscores the need for clear protocols to protect student privacy in digital environments.

**Local RRL #6**
Caillan, N. R. (2026). Analysis of Social Media and Privacy-Related Anxiety Among College Students. *Journal of Learning and Development Studies*. https://al-kindipublishers.org/index.php/jlds/article/view/12211
**Summary:** Analyzes how privacy concerns on digital platforms contribute to student anxiety. It suggests that providing anonymous channels can alleviate fears associated with seeking help online.

**Local RRL #7**
Josol, L., & Albon, K. (2025). Online privacy concerns, impacts and coping strategies among college students in Digos City, Philippines. *Asian Journal of Education and Social Studies*. https://journalajess.com/index.php/AJESS/article/view/1498
**Summary:** A phenomenological study exploring the deep-seated fears Filipino students have regarding digital data theft. It highlights that privacy is the #1 factor in adopting school-based digital services.

**Local RRL #8**
Salamat, R. (2023). Mental Health Literacy and Digital Help-Seeking Behavior among Students in Northern Mindanao. *Bukidnon State University Research Journal*.
**Summary:** This study is highly relevant to NBSC students, exploring how local digital literacy levels influence willingness to use online counseling. It found a preference for anonymous text-based platforms over video sessions.

**Foreign RRL #5**
Hamdulay, S. A., Bhardwaj, A. B., & Gupta, R. (2023). Influence of anonymity on online Counseling platforms amongst Young adults. *IEEE ISPCC*. https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10131883/
**Summary:** Quantifies how anonymity influences the willingness of young adults to use online counseling. Findings show that anonymity significantly reduces the stigma and embarrassment of seeking help.

**Foreign RRL #6**
Varma, J., Prabhakaran, A., & Sharma, H. (2022). Addressing confidentiality and privacy barrier to mental health help-seeking: an experience. *Indian Journal of Psychological Medicine*. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/02537176211056365
**Summary:** Discusses privacy-focused digital counseling in a university setting. It highlights that student trust is linked to the transparency and strength of the system's confidentiality measures.

**Foreign RRL #7**
Gurung, A., Rai, C. K., et al. (2025). Mental Health Needs of International Students: Insights for Digital Solutions. *IEEE FIE*. https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11328367/
**Summary:** Explores the need for anonymity among students from cultures with high mental health stigma. It argues for the integration of secure, identity-protected features in student support apps.

**Foreign RRL #8**
Feldstern, N. (2022). Assessing anonymity: privacy in online mental healthcare and support groups. *Tulane Journal of Technology*. https://heinonline.org/hol-cgi-bin/get_pdf.cgi?handle=hein.journals/tuljtip24&section=4
**Summary:** Performs a technical assessment of anonymity within online mental health frameworks. It provides a foundational understanding of balancing data collection with the user's right to privacy.

**Local RRL #12**

**Local RRL #13**

Impact Analysis of Cloud Security Risks in Philippine State Universities. *ResearchGate*. https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Vincent-Borja/publication/396745891_IMPACT_ANALYSIS_OF_CLOUD_SECURITY_RISKS_AND_VULNERABILITIES_IN_PHILIPPINE_STATE_UNIVERSITIES_FOR_DEVELOPING_RISK_MITIGATION_STRATEGIES/links/68f82a21e7f5f867e6e166f1/IMPACT-ANALYSIS-OF-CLOUD-SECURITY-RISKS-AND-VULNERABILITIES-IN-PHILIPPINE-STATE-UNIVERSITIES-FOR-DEVELOPING-RISK-MITIGATION-STRATEGIES.pdf

**Foreign RRL #9**
Galvão, A., et al. (2024). Ethical and legal aspects of cybersecurity in health apps. *Information Systems Security*. https://aris-journal.com/aris/index.php/journal/article/view/45
**Summary:** Examines the frameworks governing cybersecurity in mental health apps. It emphasizes "security by design" to protect users against unauthorized access and data breaches.

**Foreign RRL #10**
Inkster, B., et al. (2023). Cybersecurity: a critical priority for digital mental health. *Frontiers in Digital Health*. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2023.1242264/full
**Summary:** Argues that cybersecurity is often neglected in digital mental health research. It calls for a multi-disciplinary approach to ensure apps are technically secure as well as clinically effective.

**Foreign RRL #11**
Surani, A., et al. (2023). Security and privacy of digital mental health: an analysis of apps. *SSRN*. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4469981
**Summary:** Performs a technical analysis of security features in existing apps. It identifies gaps in encryption and provides a checklist for essential security features in future development.

**Foreign RRL #12**
Morhunova, T. (2024). *Cybersecurity in remote psychological assistance: risks and data protection*. Athens Institute. https://books.google.com/books?hl=en&lr=&id=EaYyEQAAQBAJ&oi=fnd&pg=PA231
**Summary:** Outlines cybersecurity risks in remote psychological support. It provides guidelines for end-to-end encryption and secure authentication to safeguard client interactions.

**Foreign RRL #16**

### Subtopic: Remote Counseling Efficiency & Impact

**Local RRL #16**

Mental Health Counseling: An Online Chat vs Face-to-Face Comparison. *Guidance Journal*. https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Paulito-Hilario/publication/386729304_Mental_Health_Counseling_An_Online_Chat_vs_Face-to-Face_Comparison/links/6758f29b138b414414d54f4f/Mental-Health-Counseling-An-Online-Chat-vs-Face-to-Face-Comparison.pdf
**Summary:** Finds that chat-based counseling is equally effective as face-to-face sessions for many Filipino students, while offering greater convenience and accessibility.

**Local RRL #18**
Tangco-Siason, A. (2022). Guiding from the Distance: Counselling Experiences During the Pandemic. *JPSP*. https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Amabel-Siason/publication/377471939_Guiding_from_the_Distance_Counselling_Experiences_During_the_Pandemic/links/65a8cc83ee1e1951fbbcaedc/Guiding-from-the-Distance-Counselling-Experiences-During-the-Pandemic.pdf
**Summary:** Documents the experiences of Filipino counselors transitioning to remote work, providing insights into maintaining empathy and building rapport via digital media.

**Local RRL #19**
Eroles, C. P. (2026). Navigating Support Online: A Phenomenological Study of Filipino Counselees' Experiences. *OJSS*. https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Charmaine-Eroles/publication/399717820_Navigating_Support_Online_A_Phenomenological_Study_of_Filipino_Counselees'_Experiences_with_Virtual_Guidance_Counseling/links/6969b66c5cc49c35ce7e7698/Navigating-Support-Online-A-Phenomenological-Study-of-Filipino-Counselees-Experiences-with-Virtual-Guidance-Counseling.pdf

**Local RRL #20**
Dela Cruz, E. N. M., et al. (2023). Narratives of online mental health counselling among Filipino adults. *CPR*. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/capr.12592

**Foreign RRL #17**
Cerolini, S., et al. (2023). Psychological counseling among university students worldwide: Systematic review. *MDPI*. https://www.mdpi.com/2254-9625/13/9/133

**Foreign RRL #19**
Hersch, E., et al. (2024). Remote-delivered services: mixed-methods survey of college center clinicians. *JACH*. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07448481.2022.2038178

**Foreign RRL #20**
Newcombe, C., & Broglia, E. (2025). Comparison of outcomes from a university counselling service: remote therapy. *CPR*. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/capr.12821
